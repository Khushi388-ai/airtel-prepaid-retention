# 02. Stakeholders and Requirements Elicitation Questions

## Stakeholders
| Stakeholder | What they care about |
|---|---|
| Prepaid customers (price-sensitive) | Affordable plans, no surprise price jumps |
| Retention / CRM team | Fewer customers leaving, win-back offers, customer lifetime value |
| Pricing and product managers | ARPU growth without losing subscribers |
| Finance | Revenue, ARPU, quarterly results |
| Customer care | Complaint volumes after plan changes, clear answers for callers |
| Retail and recharge partners | Sales volume, commission, customer questions at the counter |
| Network / IT team | Systems and data to flag at-risk customers |
| Competitors (Jio, Vi) | Benefit when Airtel loses customers (not an interviewee) |

## Elicitation questions
Questions ask about behaviour and numbers, not opinions or yes/no answers.

| Stakeholder | Question | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| Prepaid customers | When your Rs 299 plan was removed, what did you do (move to Rs 349, move to a cheaper plan, port out, other), and what was the main reason? | Real behaviour and the main driver of exits |
| Retention / CRM | Between 12 Aug and 30 Sep, which segments (by plan, usage level or tenure) showed the highest churn compared with the previous quarter, and what data confirms it? | Which segment is most at risk |
| Pricing and product | What churn level would cancel out the ARPU gain from removing the Rs 299 plan, and was that break-even set before the change? | Whether the trade-off was planned |
| Finance | What was monthly revenue from Rs 299 users before 12 Aug, how much did we keep after they moved, and how much did we lose from those who left? | Net revenue impact |
| Customer care | From 12 Aug to 30 Sep, what were the top 5 complaint categories (price, no notice, plan confusion, data reduction, other) and how many contacts fell into each? | Price versus communication as root cause |
| Network / IT | What customer signals do we already capture (recharge gaps, shrinking recharge value, usage drops, complaint contacts), and can any combination flag a customer likely to leave within 30 days? | Feasibility of early warning |

## Hypotheses to test with the answers
1. The price rise on the entry plan was the main driver of exits.
2. Low-usage customers did not value the extra data and left.
3. Customers learned of the change at recharge time, with no notice.
